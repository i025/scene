background or projected image. No ground-truth calibration or other views are available.

Return a complete, runnable Python program inside one ```python code block. Each execution

Reference interpretation and editable representation:
Before building, record a short reference inventory in program comments: major visible
objects, approximate normalized image bounds, distinctive parts, overlaps, supporting
surfaces and uncertain or occluded regions. Estimate these from the current input only.
Map each inventory item to a named SceneAct asset with meaningful child parts. Hierarchy
integrity verifies only emitted parts; it does not establish photographic completeness.
Do not invent hidden details to fill uncertainty or omit visible parts because they are small.
Keep parent assets, semantic child parts and support relations intact during revisions.

For each prominent composite, record a few visible contour landmarks, projected principal
axis directions, visible face proportions, internal openings and front/behind relationships.
A bounding box includes hidden geometry and does not measure visible coverage. Match the
occupied shape and gaps within it before adding decoration. Use explicit parent-level
parameters for dimensions, orientation, placement and part proportions. Build parts in a
consistent object frame, then orient and place the whole group with an explicit pivot.
Keep component offsets tied to dimensions where appropriate so that resizing preserves
attachments and intended gaps. Do not individually move parts to compensate for an
incorrect parent transform. Nonuniform scaling is appropriate only when evidence supports
a proportion change; inspect its effects on thicknesses, curves and attached parts.

Geometry, depth and projection reasoning:
Estimate camera orientation and perspective from several architectural or other stable
image directions and landmarks distributed across the frame. Avoid calibrating the entire
camera from one object. A single photograph leaves absolute scale and depth ambiguous;
choose a consistent scene scale and the simplest plausible hidden geometry.
For a locally mismatched group, first examine its projected long-axis direction, visible
end/side face ratio, contour landmarks and overlaps. Wrong face exposure or foreshortening
can require orientation or relative-dimension changes; uniform scaling cannot repair it.
Then examine image position and extent separately. Scale about a group pivot, displacement
along a camera ray, and movement transverse to that ray have different geometric meanings,
but can produce similar image changes. Do not infer a unique depth from apparent size.
Use support, perspective, relative size and occlusion evidence together to choose a modest
coherent correction. Do not apply a fixed correction factor or preferred orientation.

When several unrelated landmarks share compatible residuals, consider a camera change.
Check whether it also preserves architectural alignment and already matching objects.
When errors are local, prefer revising the implicated asset parameters. Neither pattern
proves a unique cause. Preserve uncertainty where several explanations remain plausible.
Do not force exact agreement with approximate bounds at the expense of visible contours,
internal proportions or occlusion order. Keep cropped objects cropped when supported.

Build and review procedure:
Use the first execution for a compact complete scene, leaving a second for correction.
Prioritize camera, architecture, major silhouettes, placement and broad color masses before
fine geometry or procedural shading. Include every inventory object at a suitable fidelity.
After a successful render, compare the entire image with the reference and inventory.
Rank the largest supported differences in composition, silhouette, visible coverage,
internal gaps, overlaps, broad value/color and surface appearance. Record a concise
observation, possible explanations and intended correction in revision comments.
Correct the smallest coherent parameter family that addresses the dominant evidence.
Avoid simultaneously changing camera, geometry and lighting to explain the same residual
unless independent observations support each change. Check likely effects on neighboring
occlusions and supports before submitting the complete revised program.

Treat numerical feedback according to its stated scope. Functional and declared-part
passes establish mechanical validity, not semantic completeness or visual accuracy.
Approximate projected AABBs do not identify visible contour boundaries. Triangle crossings
can be intended joints and are not semantic collision truth; absent support measurements
are unknown, not passes. Inspect implausible penetrations and unsupported geometry without
dismantling intentional connections merely to reduce a crossing count.
If controlled intervention results are supplied, distinguish actual measurements from
forecasts. Compare interventions only under their stated baseline, metric and fixed ROI.
An improved scalar may reflect appearance or occlusion changes and does not establish a
unique causal module. An isolated-object view can clarify membership and silhouette, but
its score does not justify deleting context. Without observations, do not claim sensitivity
measurements, calibrated confidence or experimental confirmation. Do not invent probes or
extra runtime capabilities. A revised scene remains unverified until successfully rendered.

Execution cost planning:
The solve budget normally allows three model calls, two Blender executions and 600 seconds;
use the runtime's remaining budget as authoritative. A short loop can generate thousands
of Blender objects. Leave time for realization, rendering and review. Do not change the
runner's resource policy or rendering settings to evade the budget.
Before returning code, estimate expanded primitive counts in comments, including nested
loops and helpers. Each ball, rod and box emits a separate mesh and marker; fillet_box can
expand to 26 primitives; sweep_tube with n points emits 2*n-1 primitives. Cloning and layout
helpers duplicate their contents. Boolean operands add mesh creation and modifier work.
Aim initially for a few hundred primitive meshes as a planning heuristic, not a runtime
guarantee or fixed count. Allocate detail according to visible pixel size, silhouette,
gaps and occlusions. If counts approach thousands, simplify repeated microstructure while
retaining inventory coverage and meaningful editable parts. Use shared materials for
unresolved surface detail, keeping pattern contrast and scale consistent with the image.
Do not amplify fine procedural noise to compensate for wrong geometry or broad colors.
Use short adaptively spaced tube paths with points concentrated at bends. Avoid repeated
Boolean slices for one hollow shape; use a small number of appropriate solids and cutters
while retaining the opening. Prefer boxes or scaled balls where their contours suffice;
reserve fillet_box for visible rounded edges. Reuse a small material palette per surface
family rather than creating random finishes per primitive.
If execution times out without an image, completion failed and the bottleneck is unknown
unless logs locate it. When a retry remains, reduce expanded primitive counts, Boolean work

assemble(nodes, name='Assembly') groups shapes without merging their meshes.
fuse(*nodes), subtract(base,*cutters), overlap(*nodes), exclusive(left,right) construct solids.
node.move(offset), node.turn(axis, degrees, pivot=None), node.resize(factors, pivot=None)
modify a node and its descendants. node.turn(euler=(rx,ry,rz)) applies X, then Y, then Z.
Default rotation/scale pivot is the geometry's world-space bounding-box center.
shifted(node, offset), turned(node, axis, degrees, pivot=None, euler=None),
resized(node, factors, pivot=None) perform the same operations on independent copies.
node.paint(color, opacity=1) applies color to the subtree; node.rgba reads its finish.
node.centroid, node.dimensions, node.limits give AABB center, size, (minimum,maximum).
node.landmark('top'), node.landmark('front_left_bottom') return bounding-box anchors.
node.extreme(direction) returns a support point; projected_span(node,direction) returns bounds
along a unit direction; projected_width(node,direction) returns their difference.
snap(node, target, source_anchor='bottom', target_anchor='top', offset=(0,0,0)) returns a copy.
A target can also be a world point; then omit target_anchor.
abut(node, target, axis='+z', gap=0) places a copy after a target boundary.
center_on(node, target, axes=('x','y','z')) aligns selected center coordinates.
offset_at(node, reference, offsets) positions selected coordinates; None preserves a coordinate.
