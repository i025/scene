Reference analysis and editable construction:
Before building, record a short reference inventory in program comments: major visible
objects, approximate normalized image bounds, distinctive visible parts, overlaps, and
uncertain or occluded regions. Estimate these from the current input, not from a stored scene.
Map each inventory item to a named SceneAct asset with meaningful child parts. Keep object
identity, child membership, materials and support relations editable. A valid hierarchy
verifies only the parts emitted; it does not establish completeness against the photograph.
Do not invent hidden details to fill uncertainty or discard visible parts merely for being small.
Use simple representations for small visible parts when their detail cannot be resolved.

Estimate camera and layout jointly before fine modeling. Record several high-confidence
image anchors distributed across the frame: structural corners, line directions, opening
boundaries and contact points where visible. Distinguish confidently observed image features
from uncertain depth and physical dimensions. Choose a convenient world scale; a single
image does not determine absolute dimensions. Fit perspective, vertical placement, framing
and relative object footprints together. Architectural anchors constrain the camera but their
inferred geometry can also be wrong; do not treat them as exact calibration.

For each large composite, compare more than its outer bounds: visible face widths and
heights, long-axis direction, near/far edge slopes, foreshortening, occupied space, gaps and
which parts occlude which. An incorrect orientation can produce the correct bounding box.
A scale change cannot generally repair the ratio of visible faces or the direction of edges.
Infer uncertain geometry conservatively from these constraints instead of defaulting to a
canonical front view or symmetric silhouette. Represent curved contours with a compact
set of shapes whose union follows the observed contour, avoiding unnecessary bulges.
Keep placement, orientation, dimensions, component proportions and material parameters
explicit and separate. Use explicit pivots for important group transformations. Apply
transforms to semantic groups so children remain attached; account for support contact
when changing scale, orientation or position.

Comparison and revision under ambiguity:

Complexity and budget:
The entire solve has three model calls, two Blender executions and 600 seconds; honor the
remaining budget reported by the runtime. Plan for a complete first render, correction and
review. Do not change the runner's resource policy or rendering settings to evade this budget.
Use geometry complexity as the controllable cost. Prioritize projected contours, major planes,
openings, occlusions and large color regions before surface decoration.
