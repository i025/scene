Reference inventory and camera reasoning:
Before building, record a short reference inventory in program comments: major visible
objects, approximate normalized image bounds, distinctive visible parts, overlaps, and
uncertain or occluded regions. These estimates must come from the current input.
Map each inventory item to a named SceneAct asset with meaningful child parts. A valid
hierarchy verifies only emitted parts; it does not establish image completeness.
Do not invent hidden details to fill uncertainty or omit visible parts because they are small.

Record a compact set of image landmarks distributed across the frame and apparent depth.
Include structural line directions, near and far silhouettes, visible border intersections,
and recognizable endpoints where available. Distinguish an object that is actually cropped
in the photograph from one that should remain visible. Do not assume the image center is
the horizon or the camera target. Estimate horizon and convergence from multiple consistent
line families when available; mark ambiguous evidence uncertain. Do not force a symmetric
layout or centered view when the image supports asymmetry.

Choose a consistent arbitrary world scale. Keep camera position, target, lens, shift and
major scene dimensions explicit. Reason about camera height and orientation together with
field of view and scene depth. Aiming at a chosen world point couples pitch to camera and
target heights and horizontal separation. Focal length changes field of view; it does not
independently correct viewing direction. Camera translation also changes parallax. These
parameters can compensate for one another in a single view, so prefer the simplest plausible
configuration consistent with several independent image constraints.

Before expanding repeated objects and fine detail, mentally project the main scene masses
and the landmark set through the proposed camera. Check that near and far objects occupy
plausible image regions and that visible border objects are not accidentally excluded.
Use the first execution for a complete scene, leaving the second available for correction;
do not spend an execution on an incomplete calibration scene. Repeated groups may share
construction parameters, but their count, spacing and variation must follow visible evidence.
Do not impose a uniform grid merely because it is easy to generate.

For composite objects, match silhouette and the distribution of occupied space and gaps
before adding fine repeated details. Estimate component sizes relative to their parent and
nearby objects. A correct outer bounding box alone does not ensure correct internal density,
component proportions or visible coverage. Keep these parameters explicit and editable.

Rendered review and correction:
After a successful render, compare the reference inventory against the full rendered view:
missing or extra visible parts, border clipping, silhouette, gaps, relative sizes, overlaps,
structural line directions and image bounds. Review near and far regions and both sides of
the frame. Approximate projected bounding boxes are supporting evidence; they do not measure
visible silhouettes or account fully for occlusion. Inspect the image as well.

Identify the largest supported discrepancies before revising. If several independent landmarks
share a coherent projection error, consider a camera correction. If most landmarks match and
one region differs, consider local position, depth, dimensions or orientation. Neither pattern
proves a unique cause. A border mismatch alone is insufficient reason to move the whole camera.
Check whether candidate camera changes would disrupt already matching regions. Conversely,
do not distort many independent assets to compensate for one plausible shared camera error.

For the available correction, choose a small coherent parameter change with an explicit
qualitative prediction for both mismatching and matching landmarks. Avoid simultaneously
changing camera, unrelated geometry and materials without separate evidence. If a camera
revision is supported, resolve that shared projection discrepancy before interpreting residual
local placement errors. Retain matching objects, semantic groups and support relationships.
Record the intended correction and remaining uncertainty briefly in program comments.
After execution, check the predicted visual effects when a rendering and review budget exist.
An unexecuted revision or an unreviewed render is not verified improvement.

Treat material appearance, exposure, illumination, texture frequency and rendering grain as
separate possible contributors to image mismatch. Do not infer a geometric cause from grain

Execution cost planning:
The entire solve has three model calls, two Blender executions and 600 seconds. Use the
runtime's remaining budget to decide whether another build is possible. A short Python loop
can generate thousands of Blender objects before any image exists. Plan a compact complete
first build so realization and the runner's render can finish with time for review. Do not
change the runner's resource policy or rendering settings to evade this budget.

Before returning code, estimate expanded primitive counts in comments, including nested

layout helpers duplicate their contents. Boolean operands also incur mesh creation and
modifier work. Count these expansions, not just named assets.
Aim initially for a few hundred primitive meshes across the whole scene, as a planning
heuristic rather than a measured runtime guarantee or fixed object count. Allocate detail

simplify repeated microstructure before execution while retaining inventory objects and
meaningful editable parts. Represent unresolved surface detail with shared materials and
restrained procedural shading; retain geometry that changes contours, openings or occlusions.
Avoid nested per-element decoration below image resolution. Use short, adaptively spaced

approximate one hollow shape; use a small number of appropriate solids and cutters.

assemble(nodes, name='Assembly') groups shapes without merging their meshes.
fuse(*nodes), subtract(base,*cutters), overlap(*nodes), exclusive(left,right) construct solids.
node.move(offset), node.turn(axis, degrees, pivot=None), node.resize(factors, pivot=None)
modify a node and its descendants. node.turn(euler=(rx,ry,rz)) applies X, then Y, then Z.
Default rotation/scale pivot is the geometry's world-space bounding-box center.
shifted(node, offset), turned(node, axis, degrees, pivot=None, euler=None),
resized(node, factors, pivot=None) perform the same operations on independent copies.
node.paint(color, opacity=1) applies color to the subtree; node.rgba reads its finish.
node.centroid, node.dimensions, node.limits give AABB center, size, (minimum,maximum).
node.landmark('top'), node.landmark('front_left_bottom') return bounding-box anchors.
node.extreme(direction) returns a support point; projected_span(node,direction) returns bounds
along a unit direction; projected_width(node,direction) returns their difference.
snap(node, target, source_anchor='bottom', target_anchor='top', offset=(0,0,0)) returns a copy.
A target can also be a world point; then omit target_anchor.
abut(node, target, axis='+z', gap=0) places a copy after a target boundary.
center_on(node, target, axes=('x','y','z')) aligns selected center coordinates.
offset_at(node, reference, offsets) positions selected coordinates; None preserves a coordinate.
repeat_line(nodes, axis='+x', spacing=1) uses equal center spacing, keeping the first fixed.
