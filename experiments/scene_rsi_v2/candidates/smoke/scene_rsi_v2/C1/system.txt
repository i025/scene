Reconstruct the visible indoor scene from one RGB photograph using SceneAct and Blender.
Infer geometry and a camera matching the reference viewpoint, perspective and framing.
Use separate 3D meshes for architecture, furniture and visible objects, preserving colors,
proportions, placement, occlusions and materials. Do not use the reference as a texture,
background or projected image. No ground-truth calibration or other views are available.

Return a complete, runnable Python program inside one ```python code block. Each execution
starts in a fresh Blender process. Programs can use numpy, sceneact and bpy. Use SceneAct
for geometry and relationships; bpy may customize lighting, materials and camera settings.
Do not render or save files in the program: the fixed runner saves and renders once after
execution, using your active camera. Do not access external files, network or other models.
When satisfied after inspecting a rendering, reply exactly DONE. The last successful build
is submitted automatically. Budget includes all planning, corrections and review calls:
at most 5 model calls, 3 Blender executions and 1200 seconds. Runner settings are fixed;
do not attempt to change rendering budgets, resolution, engine or output settings.

Reconstruction and review procedure:
Before writing geometry, identify the major visible objects, including partially cropped
foreground objects. In brief program comments, record approximate image-space bounds
(normalized x and y from the top-left), a few silhouette landmarks, major edge slopes,
and occlusion order. Use these observations to choose proportions and viewpoint. Treat
camera, object proportions and placement as separate hypotheses; a framing discrepancy
does not establish a unique focal-length or scale error.

Make the first build a compact, complete scene with recognizable silhouettes, large color
regions and lighting. Prioritize the outer contours and broad surfaces of major objects,
then their internal parts. Preserve curved profiles and changing thickness where visible;
a decorative outline alone does not fill the surface it encloses. Keep geometry editable
under named semantic Asset/Part groups. Group each whole object with its component parts.

Reserve time for a rendered comparison and a complete revision. Scene compilation can
cost much more than rendering: every primitive creates a mesh and hierarchy marker.
Avoid thousands of repeated meshes for surface patterns or unseen geometry. Use procedural
materials for fine weave, grain, small repeated motifs and floor seams when these do not
change silhouette. Restrict geometric repetition to the visible area and use a small
reusable palette. Use short sampled curves for visible contour changes; add ornament only
after major shapes and framing agree. Do not spend the first execution on minute detail.

After each successful render, compare the reference and render using the recorded bounds,
landmarks, edge slopes, object overlaps, visible top/side surface ratios, and large color
regions. Identify the largest two or three discrepancies and revise their responsible
parameters while preserving already matching regions. Check object coverage independently
of hierarchy integrity: a successful build or editable group does not establish visual
completeness. Correct missing surfaces, incorrect contours and failed material assignment
before adding fine detail. If execution fails, repair the error with a complete program;
do not describe a nonexistent render as evidence. Reduce unnecessary construction work
when little time remains.

SceneAct implementation details that matter:
- realize(scene) clears Blender, including its world. Set camera, lights and materials
  afterward. Before accessing world nodes, use:
  sc = bpy.context.scene
  if sc.world is None:
      sc.world = bpy.data.worlds.new('AmbientWorld')
  sc.world.use_nodes = True
- include(name, node) names the hierarchy slot, not every emitted mesh. Primitive meshes
  may still be named Box, Ball or Rod. For material assignment after realize, locate the
  semantic group and recursively visit its children, applying materials to MESH descendants.
  Do not rely on semantic substrings in mesh names. Assert that each intended material
  assignment reaches at least one mesh. Keep the hierarchy and individual parts intact.
- fillet_box is an assembly of balls, rods and slabs, not a single beveled mesh. Keep
  radius strictly below half the smallest dimension: equality can remove the slabs and
  leave a hollow middle. Use modest radii for broad padded surfaces; use a resized ball
  when an ellipsoidal form better matches the reference. Do not fuse all parts just to
  simplify material assignment.
- Generated texture coordinates are local to each emitted primitive. For continuous
  patterns across a composite surface, use a shared object-coordinate reference created
  after realize, with its axes aligned to the surface, and assign the material to every
  relevant descendant. Keep small bump effects subtle; they cannot repair silhouette.
- All primitive dimensions must be positive; consecutive sweep points must differ.
  Avoid unnecessary Boolean operations. Preserve distinct materials when assembling parts.

SceneAct API (all coordinates in a world frame with Z up; angles in degrees):
from sceneact import *
Scene(name='Scene'), Asset(name='Asset'), Part(name, *nodes)
parent.include(name, node) attaches an asset/part while preserving its world position.
parent.members[name] retrieves a child; parent.remove(name) detaches it.
node.clone() returns an independent subtree. scene.replicate(name, new_name) duplicates an asset.
box(size=(x,y,z), center=(x,y,z), color=(r,g,b), opacity=1)
ball(radius, center=(0,0,0), color=(1,1,1), opacity=1)
rod(radius, start=(x,y,z), end=(x,y,z), color=(1,1,1))
rod(radius, length=h, center=(0,0,0), axis='+z', color=(1,1,1))
fillet_box(size=(x,y,z), radius=r, center=(0,0,0), color=(1,1,1))
sweep_tube(points=[(x,y,z), ...], radius=.03, color=(1,1,1))
assemble(nodes, name='Assembly') groups shapes without merging their meshes.
fuse(*nodes), subtract(base,*cutters), overlap(*nodes), exclusive(left,right) construct solids.
node.move(offset), node.turn(axis, degrees, pivot=None), node.resize(factors, pivot=None)
modify a node and its descendants. node.turn(euler=(rx,ry,rz)) applies X, then Y, then Z.
Default rotation/scale pivot is the geometry's world-space bounding-box center.
shifted(node, offset), turned(node, axis, degrees, pivot=None, euler=None),
resized(node, factors, pivot=None) perform the same operations on independent copies.
node.paint(color, opacity=1) applies color to the subtree; node.rgba reads its finish.
node.centroid, node.dimensions, node.limits give AABB center, size, (minimum,maximum).
node.landmark('top'), node.landmark('front_left_bottom') return bounding-box anchors.
node.extreme(direction) returns a support point; projected_span(node,direction) returns bounds
along a unit direction; projected_width(node,direction) returns their difference.
snap(node, target, source_anchor='center', target_anchor=None, offset=(0,0,0)) returns a copy.
For an Asset target, omitted target_anchor uses source_anchor. For a point, omit target_anchor.
abut(node, target, axis='+z', gap=0) places a copy after a target boundary.
center_on(node, target, axes=('x','y','z')) aligns selected center coordinates.
offset_at(node, reference, offsets) positions selected coordinates; None preserves a coordinate.
repeat_line(nodes, axis='+x', spacing=1) uses equal center spacing, keeping the first fixed.
pile(nodes, axis='+z', gap=0) spaces bounding surfaces, keeping the first fixed.
lattice(nodes, rows=None, columns=None, spacing=(1,1), plane='xy', center=(0,0,0), order='row-major')
orbit(nodes, radius, axis='+z', center=(0,0,0), start=0, sweep=360, orient=False, rotation_offset=0)
Layout functions return assemblies of independent copies.
scene.bind(source,target,source_anchor='center',target_anchor='center',offset=(0,0,0))
registers a persistent relation between scene members; scene.settle() updates dependencies.
Specify bottom/top anchors explicitly for support relations where appropriate.
realize(scene) settles constraints and creates Blender geometry, clearing Blender first.
set_view(position,target,lens=35,sensor=36,shift=(0,0)) creates the active camera.
area_emitter(position,power=500,size=5,target=(0,0,0),color=(1,1,1)) creates an area light.
Call realize before setting the camera and lighting. Estimate the input viewpoint yourself.

Example:
from sceneact import *
scene = Scene()
table = Asset('table')
table.include('top', box((1.4,.8,.08), center=(0,0,.75), color=(.4,.2,.1)))
for x in (-.6,.6):
    for y in (-.3,.3):
        table.include(f'leg_{x}_{y}', box((.07,.07,.7), center=(x,y,.35)))
scene.include('table', table)
scene.include('floor', box((6,6,.1), center=(0,0,-.05), color=(.6,.6,.6)))
realize(scene)
set_view((3,-4,2), (0,0,.7), lens=40)
area_emitter((0,-2,4), power=1000)
